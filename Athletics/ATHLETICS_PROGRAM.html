Why Fascia Comes First

The fascia is a continuous web from your foot to your hip. Every time the arch collapses, the chain is broken — the load that should travel through the spring ends up hitting the fibula as torsion instead. These exercises rebuild the chain. Coach Chong Xie (HFT) reverse-engineered this from EMG data on elite athletes — their feet recruit differently. The goal is to train that same pattern.

Daily Protocol — Do in This Order
1
Plantar Fascia Roll
2 min / foot
›
Place a lacrosse ball or golf ball under the arch. Apply bodyweight pressure and roll slowly from heel to ball of foot. Find the tight spots and pause on them — don't rush through. Do the injured leg first.
Releases plantar fascia restriction before loading it Slow — if it doesn't hurt a little, you're not pressing enough
▶ @hft.archives — Arch & foot protocol
2
Towel Curl
2–5 min / foot
›
Lay a small towel flat. Sit with foot on it. Curl the toes to scrunch the towel toward you — then release fully. The motion comes from the toes, not the whole foot. Keep the heel down throughout.
Trains intrinsic foot muscles that control arch load distribution Heel stays planted — all movement from toes only Progress: add resistance by weighting the towel edge
▶ @hft.archives — Foot tensegrity drills
3
Big Toe Pull
3 × 30 sec / foot
›
Sit and manually pull the big toe back into extension while keeping the ball of the foot pressed flat and spread wide. Hold and breathe. Feel the stretch up through the arch and into the calf.
Activates the plantar fascia chain — the foundation of the spring system Ball of foot stays expanded, not scrunched, while you pull
▶ @barefootstrength — Arch building barefoot ▶ @zinsegrity — Barefoot foot activation
4
Short Foot Exercise
3 × 15 reps / foot
›
Standing or seated. Try to "shorten" the foot by drawing the ball of the foot toward the heel — without curling the toes. The arch lifts as a dome. Hold 2 seconds at the top, then release completely. This is the most important drill for overpronation correction.
Wakes up the tibialis posterior — the primary anti-pronation muscle Toes stay long and flat — only the arch moves Progress: standing → single leg → eyes closed

5
Single Leg Forefoot Hold
30–60 sec / foot
›
Stand barefoot on the ball of one foot — heel lifted, toes spread on the ground. Hold the position with control. Don't let the arch collapse inward. Build toward 60 seconds each side without wobbling.
Loads the full fascial chain from foot to hip under balance demand Big toe presses actively into the floor — don't let it lift Progress: add arm reaches → eyes closed

Mobility is Performance

Hip and ankle restriction changes how ground forces travel through the lower leg during cutting and direction changes — they concentrate load into the fibula. Every degree of ankle dorsiflexion gained and every degree of hip rotation opened is literally injury prevention. This isn't stretching for the sake of stretching. It's structural engineering.

Hip Mobility — Daily (10 min)
1
Leg Swings — Forward + Lateral
20 reps / leg / direction
›
Hold a wall for balance. Forward swings: kick the leg forward and let it swing back past neutral — relaxed, pendulum motion, gradually increasing range. Lateral swings: same concept, side to side across the body. Both directions, both legs.
Warms the hip joint, increases synovial fluid, opens hip flexors and hamstrings Relaxed — don't force range. Let momentum do the work.
▶ @quintin.torres36 — Hip dissociation drill
2
Hip 90/90 Stretch
60–90 sec / side
›
Sit on the floor with both legs in 90/90 position — front shin parallel, back shin also at 90°. Sit tall on both sit bones. Lean slightly forward over the front leg without rounding the back. Switch sides. Stay for a full minute minimum per side.
Targets hip internal and external rotators simultaneously — the most loaded joints during cutting Hips flat on ground — if one lifts, back off the lean Add: lean forward → rotate torso toward shin
3
Hip CARs (Controlled Articular Rotations)
5 reps / direction / leg
›
Standing on one leg, draw the biggest possible circle with the lifted knee — slowly and with full control throughout the entire range. Forward-up-out-back. Then reverse. This is active mobility, not passive stretching — you're owning the range, not just entering it.
Builds active control throughout the full hip range of motion Slow — 5 full seconds per circle minimum. Zero momentum.
4
Kneeling Hip Flexor Stretch
3 × 45 sec / side
›
Half-kneeling: back knee on ground, front foot flat. Posterior tilt the pelvis (tuck the tailbone under) before shifting forward — this is what actually stretches the hip flexor. Without the tuck you're just bending. Shift forward, breathe out, hold.
Tight hip flexors tilt the pelvis and compress the lower back — changes your running mechanics Tuck pelvis FIRST, then shift forward. The sequence matters.
5
Pigeon Pose
90 sec / side
›
From plank, bring one shin forward parallel to your hands. Lower the back leg flat. Square your hips toward the floor and fold forward over the front shin. Breathe into the tightness — this takes time. Don't rush the 90 seconds.
Deepest glute and hip rotator stretch — directly affects soccer cutting mechanics Hips square — don't let them tilt to one side
Ankle Mobility — Daily (7 min)
1
Ankle CARs
10 reps / direction / foot
›
Seated or standing on one leg. Draw the largest possible circle with the foot — dorsiflexion, inversion, plantarflexion, eversion. Slow and deliberate. Feel the end ranges. Then reverse. Both feet.
Active joint ownership — builds ankle stability in the ranges that matter during cutting Isolate the ankle — don't rotate the whole shin
2
Wall Ankle Dorsiflexion Stretch
3 × 30 sec / side
›
Stand facing a wall, foot a few inches away. Drive the knee straight over the middle toe toward the wall while keeping the heel flat on the floor. Measure progress by how far back you can move the foot while still touching the wall with your knee.
Dorsiflexion range is the #1 predictor of ankle injury risk in soccer Heel flat the entire time — the second it lifts, you've run out of range Track: distance from wall when heel lifts. Aim to increase 1–2cm/week.
3
Ankle Alphabet
A–Z / foot
›
Seated with leg extended. Write the full alphabet in the air with your foot, using only ankle movement. Capital letters, big and deliberate. Covers every plane of ankle motion in one drill.
Comprehensive joint lubrication and neuromuscular activation in all planes Big letters — small movement = no benefit
4
Bow External Sit ISO (GOATA)
3 × 60 sec
›
Sit on the floor with legs extended, externally rotated outward in a "V". Maintain arch in both feet and press both big toes into the ground. Hold the tension through the entire fascial chain — foot → ankle → hip. Don't collapse the arch or let knees cave.
Creates hip-to-ankle tensegrity chain — the foundation of bow-tension movement Feel tension from big toe through inner thigh. That's the chain working. Week 3: increase to 75 sec per hold

GOATA — Greatest Of All Time Athlete

GOATA is a movement system based on how elite athletes actually move — not how textbooks say humans should move. The core idea: the body is a spring. When the fascia loads and releases correctly, movement becomes elastic and effortless. When it doesn't, the bones and joints absorb the load instead. The Garcia Effect documented this by filming the greatest athletes of all time in slow motion and identifying the shared pattern.

The 3 Core Principles
⚡ 1. Bow Tension

The body moves like a drawn bow. The spine, hips, and limbs create tension against each other — never collapsing, always loading. The GOATA running pattern keeps this tension throughout the gait cycle so every step releases elastic energy rather than dissipating it.

🌀 2. Hip Dissociation

The hips and lumbar spine move independently — the pelvis can tilt and rotate without the lower back compensating. When they're locked together, power leaks and injury risk spikes. Hip dissociation drills train this separation so the hip does its job and the spine stays neutral.

▶ @quintin.torres36 — Hip dissociation drill ▶ @garcia_effect_official — GOATA movement pattern ▶ @garcia_effect_official — Bow-tension running
🦶 3. Forefoot Strike Pattern

Elite athletes land on the ball of the foot, not the heel. This loads the calf-Achilles-plantar fascia spring system and lets it return energy. Heel striking sends a shockwave straight up the tibia and fibula — exactly the mechanism behind both of Ibrahim's fractures. Retraining this pattern is the single highest-leverage change he can make.

GOATA Drills — Start Phase 2 (Weeks 5+)
1
Ziani Bounce
2 min after meals
›
Stand on the balls of both feet. Small, rapid, relaxed hops — barely leaving the ground. The energy comes from calf-Achilles elasticity, not muscular effort. Should feel effortless. If your calves burn immediately, you're muscling it. Relax and let the spring work.
Trains the spring reflex. Also drives insulin into muscle (not fat) post-meal. Sound should be near-silent. Loud = heel strike.
2
High-Speed Eccentric Squat
3 × 8 reps
›
Drop fast into a squat and "catch" yourself at the bottom — don't slow the descent, let the fascia absorb it. Pause 1 second at the bottom, then stand. The speed of the drop is what trains the elastic recoil response. Start bodyweight only.
Trains myofibroblasts in the fascia to absorb and return energy under load Chest up, knees track toes, land soft — no knee cave Add load only after 3 weeks of consistent bodyweight reps

Hip Flexor ISO — Knee Above Hip
3 × 30 sec / side
›
Standing on one leg, lift the opposite knee above hip height and hold it there — no wall, no support. The standing hip must stay neutral (no tilting). This is a deep psoas activation drill. If the back arches or the standing hip drops, you've lost the position.
Trains deep psoas for hip control independent of the lumbar spine Standing hip stays level — no tilting toward the lifted knee

Recovery is Training

The adaptation doesn't happen during the session — it happens in the 22 hours after it. Sleep, sunlight, cold, and ground contact are the inputs. Skip them and the training volume is wasted. For an athlete who's had two stress fractures, this isn't optional maintenance. It's the foundation everything else is built on.

Non-Negotiable Daily
1
Sleep — 8+ Hours, Pitch Black
Every night
›
This is when growth hormone pulses — the primary driver of bone and tendon remodeling. Practice nights: bed by 10:45 PM. All other nights: bed by 9:30 PM. Room must be completely dark. Phone out. This is not negotiable and cannot be compensated for elsewhere.
GH + IGF-1 secretion during sleep is the primary driver of tissue repair One night of poor sleep blunts next-day training adaptation by up to 20%
2
Morning Grounding — Barefoot on Grass
10–15 min
›
Immediately after waking, go outside barefoot on grass or dirt in direct sunlight. This does three things simultaneously: sets T3/T4 thyroid conversion (energy metabolism), establishes the healthy morning cortisol spike (focus and drive), and allows EZ water formation in the fascia (tissue viscosity). Simple but powerful.
Sets circadian rhythm, thyroid conversion, and cortisol curve for the entire day Do it before checking the phone. Literally the first thing.
3
Hydration — Mineral-Rich Water
Throughout day
›
Drink water consistently throughout the day — not in huge boluses. Add a pinch of sea salt or use mineral water when possible. The fascia's EZ water layer (per Gerald Pollack's research) depends on proper hydration and mineral content. Dehydrated fascia is stiff fascia.
Fascial viscoelasticity depends on EZ water phase — this requires minerals + sunlight Urine should be pale yellow — not clear (over-hydrated) and not dark
Structural Strengthening (Gym Days)
1
Seated Soleus Raise
3 × 15–20 reps
›
Sit with knees bent at 90°. Place a weight plate or dumbbell on the thigh just above the knee. Raise the heel as high as possible — full range — then lower slowly (3 seconds down). The seated position isolates the soleus (not the gastrocnemius). This is the most direct exercise for fixing the root cause of the fractures.
Soleus = primary shock absorber for the fibula. When it's weak, bone compensates. Full range — heel touches floor at bottom. Slow eccentric is the money. Add weight (5–10 lbs on thigh) as reps become easy
2
Tibialis Raise
3 × 20–25 reps
›
Stand with back flat against a wall, heels 6 inches out from the base. Raise both toes/feet toward the shin as high as possible, then lower slowly. Keep the heels on the ground throughout. Simple, brutal, and one of the most underdone exercises in all of sports.
Tibialis anterior supports the front of the lower leg — protects the fibula on lateral load Back flat to wall the whole set — don't arch away Add load: weight on top of foot, or use a resistance band
3
Single Leg Glute Bridge
3 × 12 / side
›
Lie on your back, one knee bent, the other leg extended or held up. Drive through the planted heel to lift the hips — squeeze the glute hard at the top and hold 2 seconds. Lower slowly. Keep the extended leg in line with the body — don't let the hip drop.
Glute weakness forces the knee to cave inward during cutting — changes fibula load path Drive through heel, not toe. Squeeze at the top before lowering.
4
Clamshell
3 × 20 / side
›
Lie on your side, hips stacked, knees bent at ~45°. Keep feet together and rotate the top knee up toward the ceiling — like a clamshell opening. Hold 1 second at the top. Don't let the hip roll back. The pelvis stays stacked throughout.
Trains glute medius — the hip stabilizer that prevents knee collapse during running Hips don't move. Only the knee moves. Add resistance band to progress.
5
Dead Bug
3 × 8–10 / side
›
Lie on your back, arms up toward ceiling, knees at 90° over hips. Press lower back into the floor — hold that pressure the entire set. Slowly lower opposite arm and leg toward the floor (don't touch it), return, switch sides. Breathe out as you lower.
Trains lumbo-pelvic stability — the base all lower body power transfers through Lower back stays flat on the floor the entire time. If it arches, reduce range.

⚠️ Load Rules — Phase 1

Reffing and pickup sessions count as impact load. If Ibrahim refs on Saturday, that is his impact activity for the day — no field session on top of it. YMCA is fine on any day. The rule is: no unplanned stacking of impact on top of impact. Both fractures happened when load exceeded what the system could absorb. This is how we prevent a third.
