## THE PHILOSOPHY (READ THIS FIRST)

This is not a standard gym program. Standard gym programs build muscle in isolation. This builds a **spring athlete** — someone whose fascia stores and releases energy, whose posterior chain is dominant, whose body moves as one integrated system.

**What changes because of this:**
- Every session starts with the feet and works up the chain. The big toe is connected to the hip. If the foot doesn't work, nothing above it fires correctly.
- Eccentrics (the lowering phase) are more important than the concentric (the lifting phase). Fascia remodels under slow, controlled load. 4 seconds down, always.
- ISO holds train the fascia to sustain tension — the elastic recoil property that makes elite movers explosive.
- Glute activation before every single session, no exceptions. Most athletes at this age are quad-dominant by default. We override that pattern deliberately.
- The cool-down is part of the training. Sustained stretches (45+ seconds) and soft-tissue release after the session is when the fascia actually adapts.

**What this is NOT:**
- Not ego lifting. Weight goes up only when form is perfect.
- Not quad-dominant machines (leg press, leg extension). These are counterproductive for the goals.
- Not grinding out reps with bad form "because it still counts." Bad reps reinforce bad patterns.
- Not skipping the warm-up to "get to the real work." The warm-up IS the real work.

## SESSION STRUCTURE (EVERY GYM DAY)

**Total time: 45–60 min**

### Block 1: Barefoot Warm-Up (5 min) — NON-NEGOTIABLE
The foot is the foundation of every athletic movement. Before touching a weight, the foot chain must be activated.

| Exercise | Reps/Time | Cue |
|----------|-----------|-----|
| Arch lifts (short foot) | 10 each | Pull the ball of the foot toward the heel WITHOUT curling toes. The arch rises. |
| Toe yoga | 10 reps | Lift big toe only, hold 3 sec, lower. Then lift all 4 toes (big toe stays down). This takes weeks to learn. |
| Big toe extension hold | 10 sec each foot | Pull big toe up and back. Feel the plantar fascia tighten all the way to the calf. |
| Heel-to-toe weight shift | 10 slow | Shift weight from heel to ball of foot. Feel the arch load and unload. |
| Calf raises — barefoot, slow | 10 each foot | 2 sec up, 3 sec down. Feel each layer of the calf engage. |

### Block 2: Hip Activation (5 min) — NON-NEGOTIABLE
Every session. This is the reset for quad-dominant movement patterns.

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Cue |
|----------|-------------|-----|
| Glute bridge ISO | 2 × 30 sec | Drive knees apart. Squeeze hard at top. Don't let lower back arch. |
| Clamshells (band) | 2 × 15 each | Keep feet together. Rotate top knee toward ceiling. Don't let hip roll back. |
| Lateral band walk | 2 × 12 each direction | GOATA position (slight hip hinge). Step — don't shuffle. Maintain tension whole time. |

### Block 3: Main Work (30–35 min)
Rotate Sessions A, B, C (see below). Don't do A twice in a row.

### Block 4: Fascial Cool-Down (10–15 min)
- Foam roll: quads 2 min → IT band 2 min → thoracic spine 2 min
- Spiky ball: plantar fascia arch 90 sec each foot — pause on tight spots, don't just roll fast
- Hip flexor lunge stretch: 45 sec each side — long hold, breathe into it
- Child's pose: 45 sec × 2 — each exhale drops deeper
- Dead hang: 30–60 sec — decompress the spine after loading

## THE THREE SESSIONS

### SESSION A — HIP HINGE & POSTERIOR CHAIN

**Focus:** Single-leg RDL, Nordic curls, posterior chain loading. The hip hinge pattern is the most important movement pattern for athletic development and glute dominance. Master it.

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Tempo | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|-------|
| Single-leg RDL (bodyweight or light DB) | 3 × 8 each | 4 sec down, pause, drive up | HINGE at hip, not spine. Back stays flat. Push the heel of the standing leg into the ground. |
| Nordic Curl | 3 × 4–6 | 5 sec eccentric | Start with band or partner assist. Just SURVIVE the lowering phase. Don't rush the concentric. |
| Glute bridge ISO hold | 3 × 30–40 sec | Hold static | Both feet flat, drive knees out, squeeze hard. Progress to single-leg hold. |
| Paloff press (band at chest height) | 3 × 12 each | Controlled press out | Anti-rotation core. Don't let the band twist your torso. Long exhale as you press out. |
| Dead hang | 3 × 30 sec | Relaxed | Spine decompression + shoulder stability. Fully relax. Don't engage unnecessarily. |

**Coaching notes:**
- On the RDL, if you feel it in your lower back, you're squatting it. Push the hips BACK, not down.
- The Nordic curl is brutally hard. Even 3 assisted reps build extraordinary hamstring strength. Don't skip it because it's hard — that's exactly why it's in here.
- Paloff press: if it feels easy, step farther from the anchor. The resistance should make it genuinely hard to stay square.

---

### SESSION B — SINGLE LEG & LATERAL STABILITY

**Focus:** Bulgarian split squat, Copenhagen plank, lateral chain. Single-leg work directly transfers to soccer, basketball cutting, and running mechanics. The Copenhagen plank is one of the most underrated exercises for athletes.

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Tempo | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|-------|
| Bulgarian split squat | 3 × 8 each | 3 sec down, 1 sec pause, drive up | Drive through the FRONT HEEL, not the toes. The back foot is just a kickstand — don't push off it. |
| Copenhagen plank | 3 × 20–30 sec each | Static hold | Top leg on bench/chair, bottom leg hanging. Don't let your hips drop. This is adductor + hip stability. |
| Lateral band walk | 3 × 15 each direction | Controlled | Already in warm-up but heavier band here. This is the working set version. |
| Single-leg calf raise (bent knee = soleus) | 3 × 12 each | 2 sec up, 3 sec down | Slight bend in the knee targets the soleus, not just the gastroc. The soleus is the primary impact absorber. |
| Hanging leg raise | 3 × 8 | Controlled | Not swinging. Initiate from the hip flexors, not momentum. Tuck at the top. |

**Coaching notes:**
- Bulgarian split squat: keep your torso upright or slightly forward-leaning. NOT straight up like a back squat. Weight in the front heel.
- Copenhagen plank: if you can't hold 20 sec, do the easier version (top knee on bench instead of top ankle). Progress from there.
- The soleus raise feels boring. It's not. It's the most important ankle/lower leg exercise in this program.

---

### SESSION C — UPPER + INTEGRATED

**Focus:** Pull patterns, pushing, loaded carries, rotational core. Ibrahim needs upper body pulling strength for athletic posture, shoulder stability, and eventually jumping.

| Exercise | Sets × Reps | Tempo | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|-------|
| Pull-up negative (or band-assisted pull-up) | 3 × 5 | 5 sec down | Jump to top position, lower SLOWLY. This builds the strength needed for full pull-ups faster than any other method. |
| Push-up (slow, controlled) | 3 × 12 | 3 sec down, 1 sec pause | Elbows at 45°, not flared. Full range — chest touches floor. Core tight the entire rep. |
| Single-arm DB row (hip hinge) | 3 × 10 each | Controlled | Support on a bench. Pull elbow toward hip, not armpit. Retract shoulder blade at top. |
| Farmer carry | 3 × 30m | Walk tall | Heavy. Long neck, glutes engaged, shoulders packed down. Don't lean. This builds total-body rigidity. |
| Dead bug | 3 × 8 each | 3 sec lower | Lower opposite arm/leg SLOWLY toward floor. Never let lower back lift off floor. This is true anti-extension core. |

**Coaching notes:**
- Pull-up negatives: if you can do 5 with perfect 5-second lowering, add a band next session and try a real pull-up from the bottom.
- Farmer carry: the weight should feel genuinely heavy. If it doesn't challenge your grip and core, go heavier.
- Dead bug: most people do this too fast and arch their back. Slow it down so much it's almost uncomfortable. That's correct.

## PHASE PROGRESSIONS

### Phase 1 — Neural Reset (Weeks 1–2: June 10–23)
- Bodyweight or very light weight (dumbbells ≤ 15 lbs)
- Priority: LEARN the movement patterns. Perfect form. Slow eccentrics.
- Nordic curls: band-assisted only. Don't force reps without assistance.
- Copenhagen: shorter holds (15–20 sec) if needed
- Track: how does each movement FEEL? Where do you feel it? Is your back rounding? Are your glutes actually firing?

### Phase 2 — Load Introduction (Weeks 3–4: June 24–July 7)
- Add weight where form is solid (RDL → add DB, split squat → add DBs)
- Track weights in a log. Every session.
- Nordic: reduce assistance. Try 1–2 reps unassisted at the end of the set.
- Copenhagen: work toward 30 sec consistent
- New addition: tempo split squat (1.5 reps — down, halfway up, back down, full up)

### Phase 3 — Intensity Phase (July 8–July 31)
- Heavier single-leg work
- Add plyometric eccentrics: drop landing into ISO hold (step off low box, land soft, freeze 3 sec)
- Add lateral bound with controlled landing: GOATA position, bound side to side, hold landing
- Nordic: 3–5 unassisted reps per set
- Jump Attack phases begin to integrate
- Dunk training: depth drops, approach jump practice

### Phase 4 — Pre-Season Peak (August)
- Max strength in key movements
- Power conversion: strength → explosiveness
- Beep test conditioning alongside
- All above plus PJF Vert Code Elite (see PJF folder)

## WHAT TO TRACK (SIMPLE LOG)

After every session, write down:
```
Date:
Session (A/B/C):
Split squat weight: ___ lbs
RDL weight: ___ lbs
Nordic: assisted / unassisted / reps
Copenhagen hold: ___ sec
Pull-up negatives: ___ sec average
Notes: (what felt good, what didn't, any soreness)
```

This takes 2 minutes and makes every session smarter than the last.
