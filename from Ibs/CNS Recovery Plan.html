Recovery Protocol

Central Sensitization Plan

Your nervous system has been in threat mode since July. This plan retrains it — one block at a time.

🧠

What's Actually Happening

▼

Your nervous system has been in threat-detection mode since July — that's 9 months. It learned that your lower leg equals danger. It's gotten so efficient at this that it's now generating signals in your face, your back, and other places. This is called central sensitization.

Your threat threshold has dropped so low that almost any input — touch, movement, attention, even answering questions — can trigger a pain response. That's why your back fired while you were just sitting and typing.

This plan works by teaching your nervous system that your body is safe again.

🦴

Can nerve pain imitate bone tenderness?

▼

Yes — you were right. This is called allodynia. Your brain has a threat map assigned to specific coordinates on your lower leg. When Amelia pressed those coordinates, your brain fired the alarm regardless of whether the bone was actually being aggravated.

Pain that moves to multiple spots, pain that appeared in your back unprompted, pain that exploded during Amelia's session as your anxiety rose — none of that is structural. Structures don't move around. That's your nervous system.

⚡

The face + chills event

▼

The shooting/zapping pain in your fibula followed immediately by your face vibrating and chills running down it — that is not a bone event. Bones don't do that.

The front of your face is innervated by the trigeminal nerve. A pain signal in your leg should not cause facial sensations unless your central nervous system is involved. This is the clearest sign that your sensitization has become systemic, not just local to your leg.

If this happens again, note exactly what you were doing, your stress level, and whether you had been thinking about pain. Bring that to the June 29th appointment.

🎯

5 things this plan does

▼

1. Accurate information — Your brain needs to learn there is no ongoing damage. Pain neuroscience education literally reduces pain intensity.

2. Sensory normalization — Gradually re-exposing the sensitized area to safe input so the brain stops treating touch as a threat.

3. Motor map retraining — Rebuilding the movement programs your brain has been avoiding since July (Graded Motor Imagery).

4. Nervous system regulation — Physiologically shifting out of the sympathetic overdrive state your system is stuck in.

5. Breaking the attention loop — Every time you scan or probe a spot, you reinforce the threat map. Stopping that behavior is half the work.

Morning Block

Before you leave the house — 6:40am
7 min
▼
Extended Exhale Breathing
5 min
▼

Inhale through your nose for 4 counts. Exhale through your mouth for 6–8 counts. Repeat for 5 minutes.

Why: The extended exhale directly activates the vagus nerve and shifts you toward parasympathetic state. You're starting your day by telling your threat system to stand down before school stress hits.
Intention Setting
2 min
▼

Before you leave, say this once — out loud or in your head:

"I am not checking my leg today. If pain appears, I will notice it and move on."

Why: Behavioral priming. You're setting the rule before the situations that test it arise, not reacting to them in the moment.

Afternoon Block

3:00–3:30pm — do this BEFORE gym or bike
20 min
▼
Laterality Training — Recognise App
8 min
▼

Download the Recognise app by NOI Group (free version). Select the foot and ankle program.

The app shows photos of feet at different angles. You tap left or right as fast as you can. It tracks speed and accuracy.

  • 1
    Open app → Foot & Ankle program
  • 2
    Work toward 80%+ accuracy before moving to Phase 2
  • 3
    If this reliably triggers pain — stop and retry tomorrow
Why: Sensitized brains show measurably reduced left/right limb accuracy. This distorts your brain's body map. Retraining laterality literally rewires the threat map assigned to that limb.
Sensory Desensitization
7 min
▼

Materials: one soft cloth (old t-shirt) + one slightly coarser cloth (towel)

  • 1
    Start on your upper leg / thigh — stroke slowly with soft cloth — 1 min
  • 2
    Move to your knee area — 1 min
  • 3
    Move to your calf — 1 min
  • 4
    Move toward lower leg/fibula area only if no significant pain
  • 5
    Never force it onto the most sensitive spot — stay at whatever level feels manageable
Why: Normalizes sensory processing by working from areas the brain maps as safe toward areas it has flagged as dangerous. The boundary moves over days and weeks.
Motor Imagery
5 min
▼

Sit or lie down. Close your eyes. Vividly imagine these movements — ~60 seconds each:

  • 1
    Walking normally across a grass field
  • 2
    Lateral shuffle, side to side
  • 3
    Striking a ball cleanly with your left foot
  • 4
    A short explosive sprint
  • 5
    Jumping and landing clean

You are not moving. Only your brain is. Vividness matters — feel the ground, the ball, the air. Dim imagery is less effective.

Why: Reactivates dormant motor programs your brain has been avoiding since July, without triggering a pain response. Rebuilds the movement maps.

Evening Block

Before bed
15 min
▼
Mirror Therapy
7 min
▼

Setup: Sit in a chair. Place your full-length mirror vertically between your legs, angled to reflect your RIGHT (unaffected) leg. Cover your left leg completely — it must be out of sight.

Perform these movements with your right foot while watching only the mirror:

  • 1
    Slow foot circles — both directions
  • 2
    Flex and point the foot
  • 3
    Heel raises
  • 4
    Ankle tilts side to side

Start at 5 min week 1 → build to 10–12 min by week 2.

Why: Your brain receives visual input of two normal legs moving. This directly conflicts with the threat map and begins to override it. Mirror therapy has the strongest evidence base in this entire plan for CRPS and central sensitization.
Wind-Down Breathing
5 min
▼

Same as morning — 4 count inhale, 6–8 count exhale. Do it in bed.

You are telling your system: the day is done, the threat is not active.

Neutral Body Awareness
3 min
▼

Lie flat, eyes closed. Move your awareness through each area for ~10 seconds in order: feet → calves → knees → thighs → hips → stomach → chest → shoulders → arms → face.

This is NOT scanning for pain. You are visiting each area neutrally and leaving. You are not checking whether something hurts. Neutral awareness only.

Why: Trains you to have non-threatening body awareness, which gradually reduces the hyper-vigilance your nervous system has developed.
These are not suggestions. The behavioral rules are just as important as the exercises. Breaking them reinforces the exact neural patterns you're trying to erase.
1

No Body Scanning

You do not check your leg. You do not press on it to see if it's tender. You do not lie in bed running through whether it hurts. Every time you scan, you send a threat signal to your brain and reinforce the map you're trying to erase.

2

The New Spot Rule

When pain appears in a new location — like your back did while you were answering questions — respond with exactly this: "That's my nervous system. It's not damage." Redirect your attention to something external. Do not press on the new spot. Do not analyze it. Acknowledge and redirect.

3

Keep a Brief Daily Pain Log

Two minutes per day. Date, location(s) of pain, intensity out of 10, what you were doing. This is data collection — not attention to pain. It shows you the trend over weeks, and trends are what matter, not individual days.

4

No Pre-Loading the MRI Results

Whatever the MRIs show, this plan continues. Even if there is residual bone stress present, the nervous system work runs in parallel. These are not competing approaches.
