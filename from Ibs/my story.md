### Key Conclusions on Pain Syndrome

The combination of responses paints a clear picture of **central sensitization** — not necessarily full clinical CRPS, but the central nervous system has been in threat-detection mode since July and has become hypervigilant. Key indicators:

- Pain that does not quiet with distraction
- Pain that has shifted to many different locations
- New pain appearing in the back spontaneously while answering questions (real-time evidence during the conversation)
- The face vibration/chills event happening without prior focus on the leg
- Pain that amplified dramatically during Amelia's session as anxiety escalated
- Nine consecutive months of injury-related stress

**Confirmed fact-check:** Nerve pain and central sensitization CAN imitate bone stress pain and create apparent bone tenderness. This is called allodynia — the sensitized nervous system generates pain at specific mapped locations when touched, and that pain is indistinguishable from structural bone pain. This is why Corrado believed the poking session couldn't have physically done what it appeared to do.

**The face event explained:** The shooting/zapping nerve pain in the fibula followed by facial vibration and chills is a nervous system event, not a bone event. The front of the face is innervated by the trigeminal nerve. A pain signal in the leg should not cause facial sensations unless the central nervous system is involved. This is the clearest sign that sensitization has become systemic.
